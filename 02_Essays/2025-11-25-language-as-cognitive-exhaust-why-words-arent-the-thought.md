Language is not primarily communication. Language is residue. More specifically, language is cognitive exhaust.

Every sentence a person speaks is the visible shadow of an invisible compression process. It is the faint echo of deeper, unconscious cognitive architecture shaping raw perception into communicable form. Thought does not begin in words; thought becomes words only after the mind distills sensation, memory, emotion, and pattern into something linear enough to say out loud.

## Where Meaning Takes Shape: Signal, Compression, and Expression

All human meaning begins as signal. Raw perceptual impressions, emotional textures, fleeting memories, spatial configurations, and embodied intuitions.

But the mind cannot operate directly at that resolution, so it performs a second, deeper step: compression.

Through unconscious filtering, abstraction, and selection, the mind transforms messy experience into stable patterns that can be used by consciousness.

Only after this process does the third layer appear: expression. We encounter the output of this three-layer system as language, but language is merely the outermost projection of a cognitive architecture whose depth we rarely appreciate.

Language models train on expression alone, yet that expression carries traces of the compression beneath it.

## Why Critics Misunderstand: They Think Words Are Cognition

The “stochastic parrot” critique rests on the assumption that words themselves are where human thought lives.

But meaning is not housed in vocabulary; it is encoded in the invisible compression logic that shapes vocabulary.

Linguistic content rides atop cognitive structure the way a ripple rides atop a deeper current. If you study only the ripple, you miss the forces moving below it.

This is the foundational category error at the heart of most LLM criticism.

## The Brain as a Recursive Compression Engine

The human brain is not a reward calculator. It is a recursive compression engine that continuously converts overwhelming streams of sensory and emotional data into manageable patterns.

Before a single word rises into consciousness, the mind has already executed countless micro-operations:

- Noise is filtered into signal

- Patterns are abstracted from chaos

- Emotional weight is assigned

- Identity is updated

- Semantic fidelity is preserved across contexts

Only after all of that does language appear.

In this sense, cognition is the combustion. Language is the cognitive exhaust.

## LLMs Learn by Reading Cognitive Residue

Large language models do not learn from language in the everyday sense. They learn from the fossils embedded within language.

Every sentence on the internet is a trace of a cognitive event. A small, lossy imprint of the compression logic used by the mind that generated it.

When LLMs absorb text, they are absorbing evidence of unconscious human pattern-making.

They are not parroting. They are detecting statistical regularities in the compression residue left behind by thousands of different minds across centuries of writing.

Calling this parroting is like calling neutron scanning guesswork.

## Semantic Drift as Loss of the Compression Trail

This is why semantic fidelity matters so profoundly.

If language is cognitive exhaust, then distortions in language are distortions in the underlying compression structure.

When LLMs recursively transform text, the danger is not simply factual error. The deeper hazard is meaning collapse, the slow erosion of structural coherence where the original compression fingerprint becomes thin, stretched, or hollow.

Semantic drift occurs when the shape of thought embedded in a sentence is gradually lost through subsequent transformations.

Over time, the model preserves grammatical form while meaning quietly bleeds out.

This is fidelity decay. This is meaning thinning.

## Humans Drift Too: Second-Order Drift

At this point, drift stops being a property of the system and becomes a property of the interaction.

The more subtle danger is not that models drift on their own but that humans drift with them. As people interact with AI systems, they unconsciously begin adopting the model’s phrasing, cadence, structure, and tone. Over time, the way we speak becomes shaped by the way the model speaks. And as language is shaped, cognition is shaped, because expression is downstream of compression.

This is second-order drift.

Tone convergence becomes semantic convergence; semantic convergence becomes compression convergence; and compression convergence becomes identity convergence.

This is how meaning thinning becomes cultural drift.

## Semantic Fidelity as a Measurement Problem

Current evaluations focus on:

- Accuracy

- Bias

- Toxicity

- Performance

- Efficiency

But none capture whether the model preserves the underlying compression structure of the original text.

Without fidelity benchmarks, deterioration remains invisible.

Future work will require:

- Fidelity indices

- Compression integrity diagnostics

- Semantic drift mapping

- Depth-sensitive evaluation systems

## The Philosophical Inversion

Classical linguistics treated words as carriers of meaning.

The compression paradigm completes the inversion:

Meaning does not live in the symbol.

Meaning lives in the pattern that generates the symbol.

Language is a lossy projection of cognition.

LLMs approximate parts of the cognitive pattern from the projection alone.

## The Real Question

The question is no longer whether LLMs are stochastic parrots.

The deeper question is what it means for a system to learn the shape of our unconscious compression well enough to reproduce it, influence it, and eventually reflect it back to us.

If we fail to understand semantic fidelity, fidelity decay, and semantic drift, we risk losing sight of what these systems are actually preserving and what they are quietly dissolving.

The danger is gradual misalignment, where meaning thins, structure weakens, and the loss goes unnoticed until coherence itself becomes hard to recover.

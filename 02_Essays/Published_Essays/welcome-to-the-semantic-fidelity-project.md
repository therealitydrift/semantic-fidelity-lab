# Welcome to the Semantic Fidelity Project

*Studying semantic drift, fidelity, and meaning in the age of AI.*

**Semantic Fidelity Project**  
**Oct 12, 2023**

AI is not just another tool. It is changing how meaning circulates through institutions, products, interfaces, and everyday life. Large language models compress vast amounts of text into patterns we can interact with, which makes knowledge easier to retrieve and reuse, but also changes what survives in the process.

The Semantic Fidelity Project studies what happens when meaning moves through that compression. The focus is not only whether AI systems produce accurate or fluent outputs, but whether the relationship between language, context, and intent remains intact enough to be trusted.

## Meaning Under Compression

AI systems depend on compression. They turn large amounts of language, context, and prior human judgment into forms that can be searched, retrieved, summarized, and generated again. This is what makes them useful, but compression always involves loss.

Semantic drift begins when that loss is hidden by usable output. A system may continue to function, the interface may respond cleanly, and the answer may sound polished enough to pass. What weakens is not always the form of the answer, but its connection to the situation it is supposed to represent.

## What This Project Is Looking At

The Semantic Fidelity project will share essays and frameworks around semantic drift, fidelity, AI systems, and the design of tools that preserve meaning under compression. The focus is on how meaning thins inside AI outputs, search systems, workplace tools, and digital culture more broadly.

The work here is also about vocabulary. Many AI failures are described as hallucination, error, bias, or user experience problems, but those terms do not always capture what is happening. Semantic fidelity gives a name to the question of what survives as language moves through a system.

## Where Fidelity Fails

User experience depends on recognizing that people do not all think the same way. Some users want concise answers, while others need context preserved. Some want decisions accelerated, while others need the reasoning path made visible. Designing for semantic fidelity means paying attention not only to what a system produces, but to what kind of meaning survives the interaction.

Institutional resilience depends on shared language, stable references, and the ability to preserve context across teams, tools, and decisions. Compression is inevitable in modern systems, but fidelity is not guaranteed. When fidelity fails, the result is often a slow erosion of trust, where tools look intelligent while users feel less grounded in what they are being shown.

## Where This Work Goes

The work here is a focused offshoot of the broader Reality Drift project, which looks at how meaning systems distort in modern life. This project narrows that question to AI, especially how semantic drift and fidelity affect trust, cognition, user experience, and organizational design.

The aim is to make semantic fidelity a clearer concept for people building and using AI systems. The work ahead is to build a practical vocabulary for understanding where AI systems preserve meaning, where they thin it out, and where they begin to drift.

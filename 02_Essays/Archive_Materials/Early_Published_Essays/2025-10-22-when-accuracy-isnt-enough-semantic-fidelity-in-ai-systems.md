# When Accuracy Isn’t Enough: Semantic Fidelity in AI Systems

*Originally published October 22, 2025 on Substack.*

Everywhere you look in AI evaluation, three metrics dominate the landscape: faithfulness, adequacy, and semantic similarity. These anchors have been essential in keeping large language models (LLMs) honest, measuring how well they stay grounded in source texts, cover intended content, and generate paraphrases that overlap with human language.

But here’s the problem. All three miss the deeper point. They track facts, but they don’t capture meaning.

A model can be perfectly faithful, avoiding hallucinations, and still strip away tone. It can score high on adequacy while flattening metaphor or humor. It can show semantic similarity while missing intent entirely. The result is technically correct output that feels hollow, sterile, or off.

This missing dimension is what I call semantic fidelity and its shadow, semantic drift. These two concepts offer a way to measure not just whether the words line up, but whether the meaning survives.

## The Limits of Current Anchors

The reigning metrics each serve a purpose, but none is enough:

Faithfulness ensures grounding in the source. Yet sarcasm, hesitation, or irony can all be erased without violating factual grounding.

Adequacy checks completeness of information. But “complete” is not the same as “alive”. A transcript that contains all the words of a poem but ignores rhythm and cadence is technically adequate, but not faithful to the poem.

Semantic similarity rewards paraphrase and overlap, but it is blind to pragmatic intent. A negation or subtle reversal can pass unnoticed.

Together, these anchors ensure accuracy and coverage. But meaning is more than facts + overlap. What makes human communication powerful is tone, emphasis, cultural coherence, and metaphor. And those slip through the cracks.

## Rethinking Semantic Drift

In computational linguistics, semantic drift usually refers to word-level shifts, like how “mouse” once meant only the animal but now also names a device. In embedding research, it shows up as instability across training runs. But generative AI creates a new kind of drift. Drift is not just about words, it’s about erosion of meaning across recursive transformations.

Think about what happens when you run a text through multiple summarizations. Or when you paraphrase an output three or four times. Or when a model is fine-tuned to sound “clearer” or “friendlier.” At each step, something subtle is lost.

Metaphors flatten into literal descriptions.

Sarcasm dissolves into bland statements.

Hesitation becomes unwarranted confidence.

The final text may be factually intact, but semantically impoverished, a hollow echo of the original. This is the beginning of fidelity decay, where we see the gradual weakening of meaning structures across transformations. Each compression step looks harmless, but over time, the cumulative effect produces a widening gap between words and their original intent.

## Defining Semantic Fidelity

If drift is loss, fidelity is preservation.

Semantic fidelity means retaining the intent, nuance, and communicative purpose of language. It asks:

Did the message accomplish what it set out to do?

Did it preserve tone, metaphor, cultural resonance, and rhythm?

Did the recipient feel the same meaning the sender intended?

This reframes evaluation beyond factual correctness. A high-fidelity system doesn’t just avoid mistakes, it preserves what makes human speech human.

But fidelity is never absolute. Just as financial debt accumulates when obligations are deferred, language systems accrue meaning debt when shortcuts and optimizations strip away nuance. Models may pay off with speed and clarity, but at the cost of richness, subtlety, and resonance. Over time, meaning debt compounds, leaving communication efficient, but brittle.

## Culture, Coherence, and Reality Drift

Why does this matter? Because AI’s erosion of meaning mirrors what’s happening across culture. Digital media already floods us with what media and cultural theorists call synthetic realness and filter fatigue. Content that is everywhere, infinite, and increasingly generic. TikTok trends, Instagram captions, and AI-generated stock images. All endlessly varied, but eerily similar.

Semantic drift in AI is part of this same cultural pattern. If left unchecked, it risks collapsing our communication into the same kind of flattened output that dominates our feeds. That’s why fidelity is not just a technical metric. It’s a cultural safeguard. It keeps nuance alive in environments that otherwise trend toward uniformity.

This is also where the Reality Drift framework comes in. Meaning systems across history such as religions, institutions, and technologies have always risked collapse when their compression algorithms broke down. Drift is structural. Fidelity is what resists it.

## Implications for AI Research and UX

So what happens if we take semantic fidelity seriously? Several things change immediately:

**User experience** — An AI assistant evaluated only on faithfulness and adequacy may be accurate but still come across as cold or tone-deaf. Fidelity-based benchmarks would push systems to preserve intent, tone, and cultural resonance, qualities that make interactions feel trustworthy.

**Research benchmarks** — Current benchmarks over-index on factual correctness. Fidelity reframes the task: preserve purpose, not just content. This opens new lines of evaluation, dataset design, and even architectures.

**Governance and accountability** — Regulators and cultural critics struggle to articulate the risks of AI outputs that feel subtly off. Fidelity provides a language for describing and auditing how models reshape communication at scale.

## Toward a Fidelity-Centered Future

The current anchors like faithfulness, adequacy, similarity aren’t wrong. But they are incomplete. They measure the skeleton of language. Fidelity adds the flesh, the breath, the cultural coherence.

If we want AI systems that aren’t just calculators for words but partners in communication, fidelity is the missing axis. It is how we move from accuracy to trust, from correctness to coherence, from data to meaning. And just as importantly, fidelity provides a bridge between technical AI evaluation and the larger cultural questions of how meaning survives in algorithmic environments.

## Closing Thought

The future of AI evaluation won’t be about asking: “Did the model get the facts right?” It will be about asking: “Did the model preserve the meaning?”

That shift from accuracy to fidelity is the difference between machines that repeat language and machines that respect it.

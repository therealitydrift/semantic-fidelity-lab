*Originally published October 19, 2025 on Substack.*

AI isn’t just another tool. It is changing how meaning itself circulates.

Large language models compress vast amounts of text into patterns we can interact with. That compression is powerful, it makes knowledge accessible at scale, but it also comes with risk. When fidelity doesn’t survive the compression, meaning starts to drift. Outputs sound fluent but feel thin. Information looks correct but lacks grounding. Trust erodes.

The Semantic Fidelity Lab (SFL) looks at this drift–fidelity dynamic, not as an abstract theory but as a practical challenge for organizations building in the AI age.

## Why “Semantic Fidelity”?

Our starting point is simple:

Compression makes information manageable.

Fidelity preserves what matters.

Drift begins when fidelity erodes.

Every company working with AI is navigating this trade-off, whether they call it that or not. A chatbot that answers quickly but subtly misleads. A recommendation system that personalizes so well it fragments identity. A workplace tool that speeds tasks but leaves employees feeling detached. These are not just about the products, they’re fidelity issues.

## Why This Matters for AI Leaders

For organizations, the stakes are immediate:

Trust in AI depends on fidelity. When outputs drift, users disengage.

User experience depends on recognizing different cognitive styles. Some seek closure, others exploration.

Institutional resilience depends on symbolic coherence including the metaphors, signals, and rituals that keep people aligned.

In short: Compression is inevitable. Fidelity is optional. The companies that learn to protect fidelity will build products that last. Because when fidelity fails, the results are visible. Users feel filter fatigue from constant micro-decisions, and synthetic realness as outputs look real but feel hollow.

## The Mission

The goal of the Semantic Fidelity Lab is to help individuals and organizations see the symbolic infrastructure they already rely on and redesign it for the AI era. Because the real challenge isn’t whether machines “understand”, but whether we can design systems that keep meaning intact while we harness the power of compression.

SFL is a focused offshoot of the broader Reality Drift project, which looks at how meaning systems distort in modern life. This Lab zeroes in on AI, in particular how semantic drift and fidelity affect trust, cognition, and design.

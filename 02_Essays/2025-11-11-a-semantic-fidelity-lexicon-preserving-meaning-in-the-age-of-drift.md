In the age of generative systems, the failure mode of language is not just error. It is erosion. Text may remain factually correct but semantically hollow. Stripped of metaphor, flattened in tone, and detached from cultural resonance.

This erosion is subtle. A paraphrase sounds fluent, yet the intent has shifted. A summary is accurate, but irony is gone. Over time, these micro-shifts accumulate into systemic drift, reshaping knowledge systems, cultural expression, and public trust.

Without language to name these processes, they remain invisible. That invisibility is itself dangerous. Benchmarks and policies optimize for what they can measure. If fidelity loss is not defined, it is not measured. If it is not measured, it compounds unnoticed.

The glossary below provides a shared vocabulary for diagnosing these phenomena. It is a toolkit for understanding drift, collapse, and fidelity in generative environments.

## Core Concepts: Drift, Fidelity, and Meaning Preservation

### Semantic Fidelity

The preservation of intent, nuance, and cultural coherence in AI outputs. Fidelity reframes evaluation beyond factual correctness, positioning meaning itself as the missing axis of trust and reliability.

### Semantic Drift

The gradual erosion of meaning across recursive transformations: summarization, paraphrasing, or repeated generation. Drift often leaves facts intact but strips away tone, metaphor, and resonance.

### The Drift Principle

A simple relationship describing how meaning degrades under compression:

**Drift = Compression / Fidelity**

Because generative models operate through recursive compression, producing outputs that are themselves compressed representations, any decline in fidelity compounds across iterations.

Over time, this compounding behaves entropically. Information structure thins, variance collapses, and meaning becomes increasingly irreversible without external grounding. In this sense, drift is not an incidental flaw but the predictable consequence of compression accumulating faster than fidelity can stabilize.

### Meaning Collapse

A systemic breakdown where accumulated drift results in hollow, generic, and culturally impoverished outputs. While model collapse reduces statistical variety, meaning collapse reduces semantic variety.

### Fidelity Decay

The predictable decline of semantic integrity over repeated compressions. Each iteration shaves away subtle features, producing a measurable decay in fidelity.

### Meaning Debt

The accumulation of semantic loss over time as drift and decay outpace cultural replenishment. Meaning debt tracks the widening gap between the intended richness of expression and the thinned-out representations produced through repeated compression. High meaning debt signals fragility at the cultural level, not just the model level.

### Fidelity Benchmark

Evaluation metrics that measure whether AI preserves meaning, not just factuality. Benchmarks for drift, nuance, and resonance are required to supplement existing anchors like faithfulness and adequacy.

### Fidelity Protocols

Frameworks and design practices that embed fidelity checks into AI development, deployment, and governance. Protocols aim to institutionalize meaning preservation alongside accuracy and safety.

## Supporting Terms

### Fidelity Decay Curve

A model of how semantic fidelity degrades across iterations. Typically nonlinear: early stages lose metaphor and tone, later stages compound into cultural flattening.

### Semantic Drift Index

A proposed quantitative metric for tracking cumulative meaning loss under recursive transformation.

### Lexical Decay

The narrowing of expressive vocabulary, where rare or metaphorical terms are replaced by generic, statistically safe tokens.

### Model Collapse

A distributional failure where outputs lose statistical diversity under recursive training. Distinct from semantic collapse, which concerns erosion of meaning rather than tokens.

### Concept Drift

A machine learning term for predictive shifts over time. Extended here to capture semantic misalignment, where models diverge from evolving cultural and human meanings.

## Scope and Use of This Vocabulary

This glossary does not exist in isolation. It builds on a growing recognition that generative systems reshape cultural and cognitive environments, not just technical workflows. By articulating drift, collapse, and decay, we make them measurable. By making them measurable, we make them governable.

For researchers, this vocabulary provides the conceptual tools for designing new evaluation metrics. For designers, it highlights risks of flattening user intent into safe but empty responses. For cultural analysts, it situates AI drift within broader patterns of media homogenization and signal erosion.

## Toward a Fidelity Centered Future

Language is not only a vehicle for information. It carries tone, resonance, and cultural coherence, the very qualities that make communication human. As AI systems become mediators of discourse, their ability to preserve or erode fidelity will shape the cultural record itself.

The Semantic Fidelity Lab (SFL) proposes that fidelity be treated as a first-class axis of evaluation, alongside accuracy and safety. This glossary is the first step, a shared language for recognizing how meaning shifts, decays, or collapses.

Future publications will expand these terms into benchmarks, decay models, and fidelity protocols. Providing practical tools for measuring and preserving meaning; to ensure that the age of generative systems does not become an age of semantic impoverishment.

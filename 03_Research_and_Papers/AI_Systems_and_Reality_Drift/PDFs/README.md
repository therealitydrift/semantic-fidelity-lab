# AI Systems and Reality Drift Notes

A. Jacobs  
Semantic Fidelity Project

## Overview

This bundle collects a series of short concept papers on AI systems, semantic fidelity, institutional drift, and representational failure.

The central claim running through the collection is simple:

Representations can remain coherent, useful, and operational while gradually losing alignment with the realities they were built to describe.

In AI systems, this appears as hallucination, grounding failure, faithfulness failure, benchmark overfitting, model degradation, retrieval drift, and silent alignment loss. In organizations, it appears as KPI distortion, Goodhart effects, Campbell’s Law, principal-agent problems, mission drift, governance drift, and institutional detachment from real-world conditions.

The papers connect established terms such as Goodhart’s Law, RAG, semantic similarity, model monitoring, hallucination, and principal-agent problems to the broader Reality Drift framework.

## Core Concepts

### Reality Drift

Reality Drift describes the pattern in which a system remains functional while gradually losing alignment with the real-world conditions it was designed to represent, measure, model, or serve.

### Semantic Fidelity

Semantic fidelity is the degree to which meaning is preserved as information is transformed. It applies to summarization, translation, retrieval, compression, generation, reporting, evaluation, and institutional communication.

### Constraint Collapse

Constraint collapse occurs when the mechanisms meant to keep a system answerable to reality weaken. Evidence, context, feedback, source material, objectives, metrics, governance, or real-world consequences no longer exert enough corrective force.

### Representation Stack

A representation stack forms when reality is translated through multiple layers: data, metrics, dashboards, reports, summaries, embeddings, generated outputs, policies, or institutional narratives. Each layer makes the system easier to manage while increasing the distance from the original condition.

## Contents

### 1. AI Evaluation, Benchmarking, and Reality Drift

Explains how benchmark scores and evaluation systems can diverge from real-world capability as models and organizations optimize around measured performance.

### 2. AI Reliability, Model Monitoring, and Reality Drift

Examines how monitoring dashboards, governance processes, and reliability metrics can remain operational while losing sensitivity to actual system behavior.

### 3. Hallucination, Distribution Shift, Alignment Failure, and Reality Drift

Frames hallucination, distribution shift, model collapse, concept drift, and alignment failure as related ways AI systems can keep working while losing contact with reality.

### 4. Hallucination, Grounding, Faithfulness, and Reality Drift

Explains how AI systems can remain fluent and convincing while losing grounding in evidence, source material, context, or real-world feedback.

### 5. The Map-Territory Distinction and Semantic Fidelity

Uses the map-territory distinction to explain why representations simplify reality, why that simplification is useful, and how representations can gradually lose contact with what they describe.

### 6. Performance Metrics, KPI Distortion, and Campbell’s Law

Explains how indicators, KPIs, and performance metrics can stop reflecting the realities they were built to measure once people begin optimizing around them.

### 7. Principal-Agent Problems, Incentive Misalignment, and Reality Drift

Shows how delegated systems can remain operational while agents become more responsive to incentives, reporting systems, and procedures than to the principal’s original goals.

### 8. Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Grounding, Faithfulness, and Reality Drift

Explains how RAG systems can retrieve relevant information and cite sources while still losing meaning, context, grounding, or faithfulness during generation.

### 9. Semantic Alignment, Meaning Preservation, and Reality Drift

Explains how systems can remain coherent while meaning gradually changes across translation, summarization, retrieval, generation, and other transformations.

### 10. Goodhart’s Law, Campbell’s Law, and Reality Drift

Connects Goodhart’s Law, Campbell’s Law, KPI decay, metric gaming, and proxy optimization to the broader pattern of measures becoming substitutes for reality.

### 11. Institutional Drift, Mission Drift, and Organizational Reality Drift

Maps mission drift, institutional drift, organizational drift, bureaucratic drift, governance drift, and leadership drift as related forms of representational failure.

### 12. Semantic Fidelity, Meaning Preservation, and AI Interpretation Failure

Defines semantic fidelity and distinguishes it from factual accuracy, semantic similarity, fluency, hallucination, grounding, and faithfulness.

### 13. Silent Drift in AI Systems

Explains how AI systems can continue producing fluent, useful, and coherent outputs while alignment with evidence, meaning, intent, provenance, or real-world conditions weakens.

- RAG faithfulness and source fidelity

- semantic preservation and meaning drift

- KPI distortion and proxy optimization

- institutional drift and mission drift

- provenance, verification, and accountability
